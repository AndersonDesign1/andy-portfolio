# Design Engineer Portfolio Research — and What It Means for andersonjoseph.com

_Research date: 7 October 2026. Method: every site below was opened in a real browser (agent-browser, headless Chromium) at 1440×900 and 390×844, inner pages clicked, text read via the agent-reach reader (r.jina.ai), quotes checked against source pages. X posts were verified through api.fxtwitter.com._

**Limits (read before trusting any score):**

- Headless Chromium does not play autoplay video or most WebGL. Raphael Salaja's grid, druids.design's project videos and Bruno Simon's world were judged from placeholders, text and structure.
- Sounds were not heard.
- X search without a logged-in account is weak. Praise quotes are few but each one is verified. "No praise found" means "not findable", not "nobody likes them".
- Scores are one reviewer's judgment on three axes:
  - **Simplicity**: how fast you understand who they are and what they do.
  - **Hireability**: case studies, outcomes, demos, CV, contact path, availability.
  - **Craft**: visual and interaction polish.

## 5. What I found (patterns across all ~35 sites)

1. **Almost nobody famous writes traditional case studies.** Proof comes from:
   - shipped open source (cmdk, Sonner, Vaul, SWR)
   - live demos inside writing
   - employer names, acquisitions and named collaborators

   Their minimalism works because people already know them. **If people don't know you yet, copying their emptiness hurts you.**
2. **Hiring managers say exactly what they want:** what _you personally_ identified, designed, built, shipped and learned; the judgment behind it; a creative use of code; tools you built. Skill lists do not appear anywhere in that.
3. **None of the ~35 sites has a skills or "Expertise" list of technologies.** Not one.
4. **The calmest, best-rated sites share a layout:** a single column 550–650px wide, Inter or a serif paired with Inter, few images, a prose bio with inline links, and email or X as the contact.
5. **Personality comes from small live details, not heavy motion.** Examples: Jhey's weather/Spotify rows, Ryo's whereabouts, Benji's "Updated" stamp, Lynn's version number, John Pham's dog toggle, Rauno's copy-email button.
6. **Contact paths are weak almost everywhere.** druids, Teathobi and Daniel Destefanis are the exceptions. An availability badge and a copy-email button above the fold already put you ahead of most of this list.
7. **Shared design philosophy:** restraint plus invisible detail.
   - "Structure should be felt not seen." (Linear)
   - "The quality of a design does not escalate proportionally with its complexity." (Shu Ding)
   - "Make 90% familiar, 10% novel." (Rauno)
   - "What's the purpose of this animation?" (Emil)
8. **Stale or broken things are noticed:** Sam Selikoff's 2022 journal, Soleio's dead link, druids' `#` links and placeholders.

## 6. Your site today — honest audit

I audited [andersonjoseph.com](https://andersonjoseph.com) live (home, /about, /projects, /case-studies/welup-digital) on 7 Oct 2026, alongside the source in `apps/web`.

### What's already right

- The type-led hero (`apps/web/src/components/hero.tsx`) is the same family as Rauno and Mike Matas: huge tight-tracked grotesk name, mono eyebrow, muted palette. It looks intentional.
- The Spotify "Last played" pill (`spotify-now-playing.tsx`) is exactly the live-detail pattern Jhey and Ryo use. Keep it.
- The case studies have real numbers. `welup-digital.mdx` has 99.99% uptime, top-5 terms and 5+ launches. `prompt-earn.mdx` has organic traffic up 250% from 10k/month and the 40k affiliate network doubled. That's more outcome data than Rauno, Emil, Paco or Jakub publish.
- The engineering hygiene is better than most sites reviewed: prefetch, view transitions, reduced-motion handling, an OG image route.

### What's wrong (facts)

1. **Your identity is split three ways.**
   - Hero eyebrow: "Founding Engineer".
   - Homepage case-study titles: "Founding Engineer & **SEO Expert**", "**SEO & Growth Specialist**".
   - About page: "full-stack developer who cares about SEO and infrastructure".
   - Tagline: "growth, interaction, and precise engineering".

   A hiring manager can't tell whether you're an SEO, a full-stack dev or a design engineer. Every strong site in this study answers "what do you do" in one sentence (Maggie: "Maggie makes visual essays about programming, design, and anthropology.").
2. **The tagline is generic.** "Building digital products with a focus on growth, interaction, and precise engineering" could be anyone's. Compare Teathobi: "Designs the product. Builds it in code."
3. **Selected Works are titled by job title, not by work.** "Full-Stack Developer at Flik" says what your role was, not what you made. There are no images, no outcome line and no year on the homepage cards. druids puts the number on every card.
4. **The homepage hides its content until you scroll.** In a full-page capture, Selected Works and the case-study images render as large blank areas because they fade in on scroll. Anyone who doesn't scroll, and every full-page screenshot or link-preview tool, sees an empty page.
5. **The Expertise section is a skill list** (HTML5, CSS3, Git, Postman…). It appears on none of the 35 reviewed sites, and listing HTML5/CSS3 signals junior.
6. **The Education column dilutes you.** Coursera certificates (Meta, UC Davis, Google) and NABTEB are listed at the same visual weight as your jobs. None of the reviewed sites lists certificates.
7. **The About page makes claims with no proof:**
   - "e-commerce client's traffic up 400%", "285% over 12 months", "Recovered 5+ websites from Google penalties", "Docker & Kubernetes", "AWS/GCP architecture", "microservices".
   - None of these has a case study, link or name.
   - The "285% over 12 months" client isn't named, so readers can't connect it to the 250% PromptEarn result or anything else.
   - Unverifiable numbers read as padding. Vercel explicitly asks for "what you personally… shipped".
8. **There is no design-engineering proof at all.** No craft page, no live demos, no interaction experiments, no components. For a design-engineer positioning, this is the biggest gap. Everyone in sections 1–2 has it.
9. **Your best story is buried.** The About page opens with how you got here: you won a scholarship to learn a tech skill for a year, picked development, became the _first developer hire_ at Welup in 2021, and taught at their academy. That origin, Nigerian and self-made, is the strongest narrative you have, and it sits in one sentence at the bottom of /about.
10. **Availability shows up only at the bottom.** "I'm currently available" is in the last section. druids and Teathobi put it above the fold.
11. **The blog is thin.** Two posts: "I built Smol" and "I updated my portfolio website". Writing is the backbone of 6 of 7 sites in group B. A thin blog is fine; a blog that looks abandoned is not. Either commit to it or fold it into a craft log.
12. **The mobile hero is tight.** At 390px, "Anderson" at `text-[5rem]` runs almost to the right edge.

## 7. What to do — concrete, in priority order

### A. Decide the one-sentence identity (do this first; everything else follows)

Pick one primary label and make SEO and growth a _differentiator_, not a second job title. Example:

> **Anderson Joseph designs and builds fast, findable web products — from Lagos, for teams anywhere.**

or the shorter druids/Teathobi form:

> **Design engineer. I design the interface, build it in code, and make sure people can find it.**

The SEO background then becomes your edge, because almost no design engineer in this study talks about performance or search. Lean into it: "interfaces that feel good _and_ rank".

### B. Rebuild the hero above the fold (hireability)

Steal from druids and Rauno:

- Name (keep the huge type).
- One-sentence identity (from A).
- **Availability pill** ("Available for design-engineering roles, Q4 2026"), reusing your existing pulsing dot. Move it from "Founding Engineer at Welup" into this slot, and put the role inside the bio sentence instead.
- **Copy-email button** (copy plus a check animation), not just a mail icon.
- **Two proof numbers inline**, such as "first engineer at Welup since 2021" and "+250% organic traffic at PromptEarn", in highlighted text like druids.
- Keep the Spotify pill.

### C. Tell the story in the order it happened

Make the arc visible on the homepage, in 3–4 short lines, not a timeline component:

1. Won a scholarship to learn one tech skill → chose development.
2. 2021: first developer hire at Welup Digital → built, then rebuilt the platform in Next.js, survived the DDoS era, taught at the academy.
3. Learned that a site nobody finds is a site that doesn't exist → SEO and growth (PromptEarn: +250% traffic).
4. Now: designing and building product interfaces (Flik, Smol, this site).

That's a real, specific, non-generic story. Benji Taylor's bio does exactly this in 5 lines. Link every company name inline (Josh Puckett pattern) and delete the separate Experience grid from the homepage. Keep the full dated list on /about, Hayden-style: one result per role.

### D. Replace Selected Works cards with work, not titles

For each card:

- **Title = the thing**, e.g. "Rebuilding Welup Digital in Next.js", not "Founding Engineer & SEO Expert at Welup Digital".
- **One outcome line with a highlighted number** (druids).
- **Year and your role** (Mike Matas, which druids misses).
- **A visual:** a looping video or device-framed screenshot (Raphael, Mike Matas).
- Make cards visible without JS or scroll. Fade in from opacity ~0.6 or animate only `transform`, so screenshots, crawlers and non-scrollers never see blanks. This also helps your own SEO story.

### E. Rewrite one case study to the druids/Bungee standard

Pick the strongest (Welup). Structure it as:

1. Problem
2. Constraints
3. Options considered (what each offered and risked)
4. Decision
5. What you built
6. Results with numbers
7. What you'd do differently

Most of this already exists in `welup-digital.mdx`. Rewrite headings as decisions, and add the "options considered" and "what I'd do differently" sections, which none of your current studies have and which is exactly "the judgment behind it" Vercel asks for. **Ship zero placeholders.** If an image isn't ready, cut the section.

### F. Add a `/craft` page — the missing design-engineer proof

This is the highest-leverage new thing. Use the Paco / Rauno / Jakub format for each entry:

- title
- date
- tag: _Prototype / Production / Essay_
- one-line technique
- 2–3 sentence explanation
- a **live demo** (React island)

Start with 4–6 entries built from things you already have:

- your Spotify now-playing pill (how it fetches, animates, degrades)
- the hero social-icon hover
- the view-transition setup between pages
- a toast, command menu or drawer built from scratch
- an "SEO you can feel" demo: the same card with and without CLS, toggled with a key (Emil's A/B pattern, but in your niche nobody else covers)

Wrap Motion components in `MotionRoot` per the repo rules. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; every reviewed craft site does.

### G. Cut and consolidate

- **Delete the Expertise skill list** from the homepage. If you need the stack, show it per project (Teathobi) or on /about.
- **Remove Coursera certificates** from the homepage. Keep at most one line on /about.
- **Strip unverifiable claims from /about** (400%, Kubernetes, microservices, 5+ penalty recoveries) unless you can link a case or name a client. Name the client behind the 285% figure or drop it.
- **Fix the mobile hero:** `text-[4.25rem]` at the smallest breakpoint, or `clamp()`.

### H. Small live details (pick two, not five)

- "Updated Oct 2026" stamp (Benji): proves the site is alive.
- Local time "Lagos · 18:42" (Daniel, Ryo): signals timezone overlap with clients, which your /about already mentions (GMT+1).
- A version number in the footer ("v3") with old versions archived (Rauno, Lynn).
- A personal principles list of 3–5 lines (Karri), e.g. "If it doesn't load fast, it isn't designed."

### I. Writing

Turn each craft entry into a short post when it's worth it. Emil's and Jakub's writing _is_ their portfolio. A realistic cadence is one craft entry a month. That beats sporadic long posts and keeps the "Updated" stamp honest.

### J. Distribution

Once /craft has 4+ entries, submit to:

- [recent.design](https://recent.design)

**Bottom line:** your engineering, and your numbers, are already stronger than most famous designer sites.
